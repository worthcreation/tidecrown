# Body and technique
Agreed with Ross, 16 Oct 2026. Health is built (build 43, 34-health); the rest is not built yet. Read before touching any skill's timing, combat, swimming, fishing, storms or health.

## The rule: no stamina game
There is no stamina bar and nothing to manage. Ordinary play never tires you and never asks you to stop, rest or eat to carry on. How far you can push is decided by your skill against the conditions, not by a resource. Push past your level and it goes wrong in a way you can read in the world: swept away, the line snaps, the fish throws the hook, you are knocked down.

## Skill against conditions
| Condition | Read against | Going wrong looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Rough, choppy, current, surge water | Swimming | worn out, swept off course, pulled under |
| A monsoon | Survival, plus shelter | knocked down, swept away, things lost |
| A strong or wary fish | Fishing | thrown hook, snapped line |
| A hardwood, an old tree | Woodcutting, plus the axe's condition | glancing blows, a stuck blade |
| A creature's tells | Strife or Accord | caught by the lunge |

## Health: the only pool
- Only harm drains it: a lunge, drowning, a storm battering. Effort never does.
- It refills on its own once you are out of danger. Food, warmth and shelter make that faster; none is required.
- It grows slowly with the knocks you survive (the pool that grows with certain actions; harder knocks grow it more).
- Shown only when you are hurt: on the body (slower, stooped) and by the screen's edges dimming as it gets low; no meter (Ross, 16 Oct 2026). Empty: you collapse (on land) or drown (in water). See Death in design-rules.

## Technique: skills live in motions
Every physical action is one of a few motions. A motion's quality comes from the skills that really train it, and transfers only where the motion is the same. Quality sets the width of timing windows, how much each action achieves, and how much risk you can carry. XP still goes to the skill you are doing (chopping: Woodcutting; slicing a creature: Strife).

| Motion | Used for | Trained by |
|---|---|---|
| Swing (axe blade) | chopping, slicing in a fight | Woodcutting mostly, Strife some |
| Knock (haft) | knocking down | Strife mostly, Woodcutting some |
| Thrust (prod) | the gentle way | Accord |
| Stroke | swimming | Swimming only |
| Reel | the fish fight | Fishing only |
| Grind | sharpening | Tinkering only |
| Haul | carrying, climbing, bracing | to be decided |

The weights live in one table in code so every link can be seen and tuned.

## Bursts
- Luck bursts (golden): chance, not performance. Frequent at low level; rarer and bigger as your level in that skill rises, keeping average XP per hour about the same. The rhythm changes, not the total.
- Skill bursts: earned by performance measured against your own recent baseline, so they never thin out with level. Their own colour and names ("In the groove", "Clean run"), so luck and skill read apart. Harder conditions (rough water, a strong fish, a storm) make clean play pay more.

## Training patterns: timing and accuracy
- Woodcutting by ear: a wobble tree's sigh is the beat (slow and even in young trees, uneven and quickening in old ones); the hinge cracks before a tree goes, the cue for the last clean strike or to step clear; hardwoods creak on off-beats. Every sound has a visible match (a leaf shiver, a split line) and a buzz where the phone can vibrate, so sound off loses nothing.
- Fishing: the hook window shrinks for wary fish; the fight has a tension rhythm.
- Grinding: steady, even strokes; unevenness slows the work, a long steady run sharpens faster.
- Swimming: stroke rhythm and breathing; rough water puts off-beat swells in the way.
- Cooking: juggling heat across pans.
- Dominion: tells that quicken and feint at higher levels.

## Motion controls
On by default, used only in specific places. For now: setting the hook (flick the phone up) and the fish fight (tilt against the pull; lift, then lower while winding). Small fish stay simple. Swipe fallback (swipe up to hook, drag left and right for tension) pays exactly the same. Never large swings. Can be turned off in settings. iPhone asks permission on first use.
